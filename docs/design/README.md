# StykAnimate redesign spec (revision 4.3, "Simple at a glance, powerful underneath")

The redesign of the StykAnimate editor: the Islands backbone and the brand (white and mist, Inter Tight and Inter, an ink pill primary, one Styk blue, concept colours used sparingly), with **every function of today's app kept at most as many clicks away (3 low-frequency trades documented) and no high- or medium-frequency function less visible than today, counted per function and clip-aware, at 1440×900, at the window sizes people use and from inside the sessions, frequent commands visible where you work, and calm through structure rather than absence**. Core systems (document model, editor commands, renderer, IK default, file formats, IPC) are untouched.

## In this repository

The spec was written and measured outside the repository and copied here with repo-relative paths. The reference editor, design tooling and concept mockups it cites under `design/` are not part of the public repository; those paths are kept as historical references. "Today's app" in these documents is `main` at `c82312fb`, where it was measured.

## Why revision 4

Revision 3 answered the owner's first message ("the layout and UI and UX is not super clean. It feels too crowded and overwhelming in most cases … keep it clean, easy to use and beautiful to look at in every way. Users should feel at home when looking at the app, even at a first glance everything must feel perfect and not overwhelming in any way") by moving power into menus. It was calm, but it hid 258 of today's functions and made 194 of them further away (the revision 3 gap analysis, internal research, not in the repository). The owner's latest message sets the standard revision 4 is built to:

> "make sure that we don't lose any functionality or ease of use or hide good buttons just to get a clean UI. The UI must be clean but everything should still be there and not completely hidden. The redesign shouldn't just make it cleaner but also improve UX across the board, improve interactions and layout in general. This isn't achieved by just not showing stuff or losing ease of use of functionalities. I want this to be perfect. A modern UI and UX that is simple to use but super powerful. It should feel simple at a glance but have all the functionality we have now and room for even more."

**Revision 4.1** answers the review of revision 4: its "nothing further, nothing less visible" held only at 1440×900 (at 1180px and below the menubar and frame rate folded, at 900px and below the inspector, view toggles, workspace switch and Simple │ Advanced did, while today's app keeps them all down to about 800px), several medium-frequency entry points were less visible than today (VFX's Apply to, Show filter and Presets, eye and lock, a library card's actions, the Tween type), the reference drew only part of today's content (6 of 14 brushes, 6 of 22 effects), icon-only rows let one glyph mean two things (the joint's Match pose used the transport's frame steps), Draw was the busiest screen, three defaults had changed, and the counts were optimistic. Everything below is measured again.

**Revision 4.2** answers the review of revision 4.1. Its gates could not see what a person sees: a control scrolled out of the inspector or covered by the timeline header counted as visible, a group's "2–3" clicks scored every function on its best case, and only a drop from "visible at rest" failed. With the gates made honest (a target must be inside every clipping ancestor and the element at its centre, a scroll costs a step; every function counted by itself; any drop in visibility fails for a high- or medium-frequency function; the message, glyphs, labels, thumbnails, menus and scope lines audited), revision 4.2 fixes what they found and what the reviewers measured: VFX puts the selected effect's own settings under the list (T38 at 6 steps at every size, as today), the joint menu starts with today's joint right-click list, Build shows *Fit figure* and names the figure it edits, *Use IK* shows the pin it makes, the one message is never cut and never meets the view cluster, the workspace switch keeps its names longest, one-step order is visible again, Draw's brushes and the effects show what they are, fields follow one pattern per kind, Save, Join, Isolate and Level carry words, and the interaction defects on the power routes are specified away.

**Revision 4.3** answers the review of revision 4.2 (the power-user, newcomer, owner and verification judges). It measured what the gates still could not see: a file with more layers than the demo's three (the default timeline showed fewer layer rows than today's at every size: 2.25 content rows at 1280×720 against today's 4.38), the sessions (the camera session hid the figures' joint handles and every session replaced Export with Done, while today's app keeps both), Draw on a new file (never drawn), labels that fold (Add frame lost its word before a disabled *Edit together*; the ink primary became a black circle at first launch at 1024px), a ruler with its zoom tools over the last frames and the playhead, the counts (a budget count compared with a different count of today's app), and the one remaining function-level regression (segment surgery, 3 → 6–7 clicks). Revision 4.3 fixes each of them, measures today's app wherever it makes a claim (internal research, not in the repository), and freezes every ceiling.

The earlier vetoes still hold: no keyframe diamonds or key state in the inspector (keys stay in the Advanced timeline, drawn as today, better); no selection ring, hover halo or joint name tags; no default changes (Advanced timeline, onion off with `#e76b70`/`#549de7`, IK off, the first figure selected); no set-length command.

## What changed in revision 4.2, and why

**Gates that see what a person sees** (the review showed the audits could not catch the regressions they reported)
- **Clip-aware and covered-aware targets.** The task gate and the tier pass (`shoot.mjs`, `whereIs`) now require every target to be inside every clipping ancestor and to be the element at its centre (a control covered by the timeline header, as T38's Halo colour was at 1440×900, fails); a target a scroll container must scroll to, or one under the inspector's fade, **costs one more step**. A large target (the timeline grid, the canvas) needs only a visible part.
